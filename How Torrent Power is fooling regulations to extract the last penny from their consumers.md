On Thursday morning, I saw solar panels being planted in my residency building. It suddenly got me thinking about the amount I might save on my electricity bill. As we had limited rooftop space, we were only allotted one panel per flat. I am not sad but rather happy. I know electricity prices increase as we consume more energy. Being a finance nerd, I rushed to get my power bill to calculate the savings on the electricity bill from next month. However, the more I research, the more my illusion breaks.

Firstly, as per table (1) below, the tariff changes based on slab are not significant enough to make sense of my above hypothesis. My energy consumption is about 180 units. Thus, comparing the first and the third slab, I was only saving about 1₹ per unit consumption. Though, it can seem like a significant reduction of about 25%, the actual price I pay has other charges such as Fuel and Power Purchase Price Adjustment (FPPPA).

So, surely, now we need to check what FPPPA is, how it is calculated, and if I can save any money there. FPPPA is an additional charge included in our electricity bill that accounts for the variation in the fuel and power purchase costs incurred by the electricity supplier. Given this I got into thinking, when someone produces excess energy from solar, the power provider buys it back at ₹2-3 per unit. So, if everyone plants solar panels and produces, even little, more than they consume then FPPPA might go down in future and people without or with limited solar can benefit from it. But can I trust these power giants, what if they are exaggerating the cost? A quick perplexity search showed me that FPPPA changes every quarter and are approved by the state board. How great is that, there are regulations protecting us. Now, being more curious, I wanted to check how much these companies buy energy for, and how much they sell for, and what are their profits? My next search gave me links to documents submitted by private power suppliers to the state board for FPPPA price approval.

With a quick look, I was amazed how Torrent power is buying energy at around ₹7 and charging ₹7.8-₹8.5 from an average consumer. Then, for our goal, I checked for excess energy purchased, however, I didn’t find any purchases that cost ₹2.25 per unit (this is what Torrent Power pays consumers for excess energy). However, wait, did you see anything strange? What is wrong with purchasing in Sl. No. 2 (from table (2) above)? Why is Torrent Power paying ₹16.545 per unit to SUGEN/UNOSUGEN?

A quick new search showed SUGEN/UNOSUGEN is nothing but Torrent’s own power plant. They are selling their own energy at very high prices to inflate FPPPA and thus charge their consumers more. Excluding SUGEN/UNOSUGEN, Torrent Power bought 2,653 MUs for 1,590 crores with an average cost of around ₹5.99/unit. SUGEN/UNOSUGEN while contributing less than 10% of total purchases contributed more than 22% in bills inflating Average Power Purchase Rate from around ₹6 to more than ₹7. Using average cost from other sellers, Torrent Power could have bought 280 million units of power at about 168 crores, saving just a little less than 300 crores for their consumers.
